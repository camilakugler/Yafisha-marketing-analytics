# Y.Afisha Marketing Analytics

## Overview

This project analyzes product usage, customer purchasing behavior, and marketing performance for **Y.Afisha**. The goal is to identify where marketing investment generates value and provide data-driven recommendations for budget allocation.

The analysis combines website visit logs, customer orders, and marketing costs to evaluate the full customer journey from acquisition to revenue generation.

## Business Questions

The project addresses four main questions:

1. How do users interact with the product?
2. How quickly do users convert from their first visit to their first purchase?
3. How much value does each customer generate over time?
4. Which acquisition channels recover their marketing investment and should receive more budget?

## Data

The analysis uses three datasets:

- **Visits:** user sessions, timestamps, device information, and acquisition source.
- **Orders:** purchase timestamps and revenue generated by each customer.
- **Costs:** daily marketing expenses by acquisition source.

The core visit and marketing datasets cover **June 2017 through May 2018**.

> The original datasets are not included in this repository. The notebook expects the files `visits_log_us.csv`, `orders_log_us.csv`, and `costs_us.csv`.

## Analysis Workflow

### 1. Data Preparation

- Inspected dataset structure and data quality.
- Standardized column names.
- Converted date fields to `datetime`.
- Checked for missing values and duplicates.
- Created daily, weekly, and monthly time dimensions.

### 2. Product Analytics

The product analysis evaluates:

- Daily Active Users (DAU)
- Weekly Active Users (WAU)
- Monthly Active Users (MAU)
- Sessions per day
- Session duration
- Monthly cohort retention

### 3. Sales Analytics

The sales analysis measures:

- Time from first visit to first purchase
- Order frequency
- Average Order Value (AOV)
- Purchase behavior by acquisition cohort
- Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

### 4. Marketing Analytics

Marketing efficiency is evaluated using:

- Total marketing spend
- Spend by acquisition source and month
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- LTV by acquisition source
- Return on Investment (ROI)
- Return on Marketing Investment (ROMI) by cohort

## Key Findings

### User Behavior

After removing sessions with non-positive duration, the analysis contains **323,604 valid sessions**. Average session duration is approximately **11.9 minutes**, while the median is **6 minutes**, indicating a right-skewed distribution caused by a smaller number of long sessions.

Monthly cohort retention drops sharply after the first visit. First-month retention is generally only about **4% to 8%**, showing that long-term user retention is a major challenge.

### Conversion and Purchasing Behavior

Among users who eventually purchased, approximately **73.7% converted on the same day as their first visit**. Conversion probability declines substantially as more time passes after the initial visit, making early engagement particularly important.

Monthly order frequency remains close to one order per purchasing customer. The overall **Average Order Value is approximately 5.00**, while the median order value is **2.50**, indicating that higher-value purchases pull the average upward.

### Customer Lifetime Value

Cumulative LTV grows most rapidly during the first few months after acquisition and then slows. This reinforces the importance of early customer engagement and retention.

At the acquisition-source level, **Source 1** generated the highest estimated LTV at approximately **3.29 per acquired user**, followed by **Source 2** at approximately **2.88**.

### Marketing Performance

Total marketing spend was approximately **329,131.62**. **Source 3** received the largest share of the budget, with approximately **141,321.63** in spending, but produced the weakest return among the major channels.

Approximate ROI by source:

| Source | ROI |
|---|---:|
| 1 | +46.6% |
| 2 | +10.4% |
| 9 | +4.2% |
| 5 | +3.1% |
| 4 | -9.0% |
| 10 | -20.8% |
| 3 | -62.5% |

Sources without sufficient matching cost data were excluded from direct ROI interpretation.

## Recommendations

Marketing budget should be shifted toward channels with **positive ROI and LTV above CAC**. Based on the analysis:

- **Prioritize Source 1**, which produced the strongest ROI and the highest LTV among the analyzed acquisition sources.
- **Maintain and test additional investment in Source 2**, which also generated positive returns and strong LTV.
- **Consider carefully scaling Sources 5 and 9**, which produced smaller but positive returns.
- **Reduce or reassess Source 3**, which received the largest marketing investment but generated a strongly negative ROI.
- Review Sources 4 and 10 before further investment because their returns were negative during the analyzed period.
- Invest in improving the first-session and early post-acquisition experience because both conversion and retention are highly concentrated in the beginning of the customer lifecycle.
